You do not need more customers. You need to lose fewer of the ones you already quote.

The Four Levers, In The Order We Would Pull Them
Lever 1 — Recover the quotes you already issued

No new demand required. No capacity risk. Start here.

Every firm that asked you to price a job had already decided the work needed doing. The estimating time is already spent. Some of those quotes were lost on price and some were lost because the job slipped and nobody rang back — and today you cannot tell those two apart, because nothing records that a quote ever happened.

Those two causes need opposite responses. One is a pricing conversation. The other is a phone call that never got made. Recovering even a portion of the second costs you nothing in capacity, because those jobs were already yours to lose.

Lever 2 — Steer the mix toward the 50% end

The single biggest lever in the business, and it is currently unmanaged.

You told us margin ranges from 20% to 50%. That spread is enormous. The same £500k of revenue is worth £100k or £250k of gross profit depending entirely on which jobs it comes from.

Nothing currently records which jobs sit at which end, so the mix is whatever walks through the door. Once quotes carry a value and an outcome, you can see the pattern — by job type, by customer, by product — and quote deliberately toward the profitable end. Same capacity. More money. No extra jobs.

Lever 3 — Give your counter its time back

This is the one that attacks the operational constraint directly.

Every small repeat order — brushes, rollers, masking, overalls, paddles — taken by hand at the counter is time your people are not spending on the thing you actually sell, which is working out systems and quantities. That estimating judgement is your product. The brush order is not.

Moving predictable repeat consumables off the counter is not an ecommerce project. It is capacity recovery — and capacity is the exact thing you told us is stopping you. It is how a business grows 25% without hiring 25% more people.

Coatings stay an enquiry, permanently. Every Jotun, Remmers and Flexcrete system needs a substrate, a specification and one of your people on the phone. We would not touch that, and nobody should be buying those from a dropdown.

Lever 4 — Be upstream of the work, not bidding for it

Longest to pay off, and the one that gets you to £3M rather than £2.5M.

You already offer CPD presentations, site surveys, specifications and demonstrations. In the specification trade that is not marketing collateral — it is the whole route to market. The specifier writes your system into the document, and then every contractor bidding that job has to come to you.

That is being upstream of the work instead of competing for it. And it selects for exactly the jobs you want: larger, specified, higher margin, planned in advance rather than urgent.

Today there is no page on your website for surveys, specifications, demonstrations or CPD. They are four words in a menu. An architect searching for CPD on protective coatings finds nothing of yours.

Why Northern Ireland Being Small Is An Advantage
Your market can be listed by name

You told us you sell into Northern Ireland only. That is normally described as a limitation. For what you sell, it is the opposite.

Every organisation in Northern Ireland that could ever buy protective coatings can be written down: the commercial and industrial contractors, the architecture practices, the engineering consultancies, the facilities teams, the councils, the housing associations. It is a countable set, not an anonymous audience.

That means coverage, not advertising. You do not need to pay to be found by people you could simply write to, ring, or present a CPD to. And you can know, at any moment, what percentage of the total addressable market in this country you have actually spoken to — which is a number almost no business can calculate.

The Open Question We Need Answered Before Anything Is Built
Warren — this one is yours

You said you do not aspire to share pricing online. We have deliberately not designed around that until we know which of two very different things you meant:

If you meantThen
“I don't want my prices public, where competitors and every customer can see them”Already solved. A trade login shows each account its own agreed price and nothing is visible to the public or to other customers. No price list exists on the open site.
“I don't want an order arriving without one of my people speaking to the customer”Then Lever 3 comes off the table entirely, and we put that effort into Levers 1, 2 and 4 instead.

Both are legitimate positions and we are not going to talk you out of either. But they lead to different plans, and we would rather ask than assume.

The Sequence
  • Step 1 — This week. Put measurement on the website. Free, twenty minutes, nothing to buy. It only records from the day it goes on, so every week without it is history you cannot recover.
  • Step 2 — Weeks 1–3. Stand up the quote list. Every enquiry, every quote, a value and a stage against each, and a prompt when one has gone quiet.
  • Step 3 — Weeks 2–4. Build the estimating page and an enquiry form that asks what an estimate actually needs — substrate, area, condition, timescale — instead of asking for a message.
  • Step 4 — Month 2. Work the back catalogue of past quotes. No new demand, no capacity risk, and it is the fastest revenue in the plan.
  • Step 5 — Month 3. First margin review. Which job types, customers and products sit at 50% and which at 20% — and what that says about what to quote for.
  • Step 6 — Month 3–4. Specification and CPD pages built, and the brochure library set up so you know which specifier downloaded what, before the tender goes out.
  • Step 7 — Month 4+. The trade counter, only if the answer above allows it and only once the quote data says it is worth it.
  • Step 8 — Ongoing. Named-account coverage of the Northern Ireland contractor, specifier and facilities list.

What We Are Deliberately Not Doing
Said plainly, so there is no confusion later
  • No advertising of any kind. No Google, no Meta, no spend on your behalf.
  • No new website. Yours is well built and fast on a phone. Everything here works with it.
  • Nothing that touches your stock or accounts systems. Not replaced, not migrated. If a piece of work would require that, we would tell you and stop rather than proceed.
  • No selling of coating systems online. Ever. Those stay an enquiry.
  • No chasing homeowners. Your customers are contractors, applicators, facilities teams and specifiers.
  • No growth past the ceiling you set. You said doubling would be too much. We have planned to £2.5–3M, not beyond it.
What We Need From You
  • 1. The answer to the pricing question above. It decides whether Lever 3 exists.
  • 2. Roughly how many jobs you quoted last year, and roughly how many came back.
  • 3. Your average delivery value — the ordinary one, not the exceptional one.
  • 4. What your stock and accounts run on.
  • 5. Whether trade prices are the same for everyone or agreed account by account.
  • 6. Roughly how much counter time goes on small repeat orders.

Items 4 and 5 move the cost of the trade counter by several thousand pounds in either direction, which is why we have not put a firm number on it. Item 3 decides how hard the whole target is. None of these are things we can find out from the outside, and we would rather ask than estimate.

The measurement goes on first, and it is free.

Twenty minutes, nothing to buy, no commitment either way. Decide everything else once you can actually see something.
