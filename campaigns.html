This is the one part of the plan where advertising makes sense — and it is not a volume campaign. It is buying the search nobody is competing for, for the service nobody offers, that produces your highest-margin work.

Northern Ireland only · specification-led · low volume by design
First — Why This Does Not Contradict The Rest Of The Plan
You told us the constraint is operational, not demand

That rules out a volume campaign, and we are not proposing one. What follows is built to bring in fewer, larger, better-specified jobs, not more of everything. Three things make it work despite the ceiling:

1 · The specification pays you regardless of who wins

You survey the building, write the specification, and it goes out to three or four contractors to price. Whichever one wins comes back and buys the material from you, because you wrote the document they are all pricing against.

That means one survey does not consume four times the capacity, but it can be worth several times a single job. It is the one thing in your business that multiplies without proportional effort — which is exactly what a capacity-limited firm should be buying.

2 · It selects for the high-margin end

You told us margin runs from 20% to 50%. Specified work is planned, larger, scheduled in advance and priced on suitability rather than on being cheapest. Advertising that biases toward specification is the margin-mix lever, bought rather than waited for.

3 · It is aimed at people who are not already ringing you

Contractors already know you and ring the counter. Building owners, facilities managers, architects and engineers do not — and they are the ones who commission a specification. This adds a new kind of enquiry, not more of the kind you already handle.

What that table actually says

One of six firms is buying search, and they are in England selling product on price. Neither Northern Ireland competitor is advertising at all.

And not one of the six — including the one spending money — is claiming the specification ground. So the search term you would be bidding on has, on this evidence, no local competition and no national competition offering the same thing.

That situation does not usually last. It lasts here because the firms who could do it are product sellers, and the firm who should do it has no page for it.

What We Would Not Advertise, And Why
Ruled out deliberately
  • Coating systems on price. You will lose that fight to an English website with a shop, and winning it would be worse — it is the low-margin end.
  • Anything aimed at homeowners. Wrong customer entirely.
  • General “paint supplier Belfast” terms. Those reach contractors who already know you and would have rung anyway. Paying for a call you were getting free.
  • Brushes, rollers and consumables — for now. There is nothing to send the click to until the counter exists, and the counter is on hold pending your answer on pricing.
  • Meta and Instagram. Your buyer is not commissioning a coating specification from a social feed.
  • Any campaign at all before measurement is installed. Spending into a website that cannot see a conversion is the exact mistake we see most often.
What We Do Not Know Yet — And Will Not Pretend To
Read this before anybody commits a budget

We have not pulled search volumes. Every volume and cost-per-click cell above says “not assessed” because that is the truth. Those figures come from Google's Keyword Planner against your actual account and region, and we would pull them before you spend anything. We would rather show you an empty cell than a number we made up.

Northern Ireland is a small market and these are niche business terms. It is entirely possible that some of these searches happen only a handful of times a month. If that is what the data says, we will tell you, and Campaign 3 becomes the lead with paid search as a support act rather than the other way round.

This channel will never look impressive on a traffic report. Low volume is the point. It should be judged on surveys booked and specifications written — never on clicks, impressions or visits.

No budget is proposed on this page, because a budget set before volumes are known is a guess with a decimal point in it.

The Order It Has To Happen In
  • 1. Measurement installed. Free, twenty minutes. Nothing can be judged before this and no money should be spent before it.
  • 2. Pull real search volumes for Northern Ireland. This is the go/no-go, and it may kill Campaign 1.
  • 3. Build the specification and survey page, and the CPD page. There is nowhere to send a click today.
  • 4. The quote list live, so an enquiry from an advert is tracked to won or lost like any other.
  • 5. Campaign 1 live on a small budget. Judged on surveys booked.
  • 6. Campaign 2 live, geo-restricted, small.
  • 7. Campaign 3 as direct coverage of the NI practice list.
  • 8. Review at 90 days against specifications written — and stop anything that is not producing them.
