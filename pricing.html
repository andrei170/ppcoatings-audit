How The Google Budget Actually Works — A Correction
You asked whether an unspent daily budget rolls over. We said yes. The accurate answer:
  • You only pay when someone clicks. No clicks, no charge.
  • Google balances spend across the month, not day by day. An unspent day is not banked and carried forward as such — instead, on busier days Google may spend up to twice the daily amount to catch the searches that are there.
  • It will never charge more than your monthly limit — roughly the daily budget multiplied by 30.4. At £1,500 a month, that is about £49 a day on average.
  • Setting it to Northern Ireland only does not make it more expensive. It concentrates the same money on the searches you actually want.

The number that matters is thirteen

Thirteen or fourteen jobs a year, by your own arithmetic, are lost to silence rather than to price. Not lost to a competitor being cheaper. Lost because a quote went out and nobody rang.

That is the entire job of Track A. You do not need the advertising or the trade counter for that number to move — you need somebody to know which quotes have gone quiet. It is the cheapest thing on this page and it is the one we would do first.

Read this before you trust any number above

The 30% and the 25% are figures you chose on the working model, not rates measured in your business — because nothing in your business currently records them. They may be generous. They may be conservative. Neither of us knows.

That is not a flaw in the argument, it is the argument. After a month of the quote list running, the 13-14 becomes a real number instead of an assumption, and you can judge everything else against it. We have deliberately not converted any of this into a profit figure, because your margin on an ordinary job is yours to state and we are not going to invent it.
