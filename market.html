The Thing That Connects The Two Routes
The 30% is the lever on the 70%

These are not two separate markets. They are the same job, seen from two ends.

You survey the building, write the specification, and it goes out to three or four contractors to price. Whichever one wins comes back and buys the material from you — because you wrote the document they were all pricing against.

So when an architect or a structural engineer writes a Patterson system into a specification, the steel fabricator bidding that job has to come to you. He does not choose his supplier; the document does.

That is why the specifier route matters more than its 30% share suggests. It is the only way to win a fabricator who already buys from somebody else without competing on price. You cannot cold-sell a fabricator off a ten-year supplier relationship. You can be written into the job over his head.

And the honest consequence for advertising

A steel fabricator does not search for “protective coating specification”. He has a supplier, he buys repeatedly, and he is won on technical support, approved-applicator standing and being ringable on a Tuesday when it is going wrong.

So paid search is the wrong instrument for most of your 70%. It reaches the specifier route, building owners, and genuine new entrants. Reaching fabricators is a named-account job, not a media job — and we would rather say that now than sell you clicks aimed at people who do not click.

Why A Small Market Is An Advantage Here
You can know your own market share

In a market of this size, coverage replaces advertising. You do not need to pay to be found by people you could write to, ring, or present a CPD to — because the list of them is finite and can be built.

It also means you can answer a question almost no business can: what percentage of every organisation in this country that could buy from us have we actually spoken to? That is a real number here, and it is not a real number in England.

And the specifier route multiplies

There are 1,000+ chartered architects in Northern Ireland and 70–80 practices in the RSUA scheme. That is not an audience — it is a list you could work through deliberately.

And each one is worth more than a contractor, because a specifier writing your system into a document sends every contractor bidding that job to you. You are paid regardless of which one wins.

The Timing Argument
This is the strongest market moment in fifteen years

Output at a fifteen-year quarterly high. Business numbers at their highest since records began in 2010. Total output up 4.3% in a year to £4.9 billion, with growth across the wider economy described as construction-led.

New build and refurbishment both generate coating specifications. More buildings going up and more being refurbished means more steel to protect, more floors to coat and more intumescent work to specify.

The argument is not that the market will stay like this. It is that the ground is uncontested right now — not one of your five competitors offers a survey or a specification, and only one of the six is buying search at all. A rising market with no competitor claiming the high-value service is not a permanent condition.

What We Still Do Not Know
Named honestly, because it is the go/no-go

How often Northern Ireland actually searches for these terms. A market being large and growing does not mean people type “protective coating specification” into Google. The two are different questions and only one of them is answered on this page.

That figure comes from Google's Keyword Planner against your region, it costs nothing, and it is the first thing we would pull. If the searches are not there, the paid campaigns come off the table and the CPD route to the 70–80 practices becomes the plan instead — which needs no media budget at all.

Also not assessed: the council and housing association framework routes, the size of the refurbishment market specifically against new build, and how much of the £4.9bn involves coatings at all. We have not estimated any of them and we are not going to.
