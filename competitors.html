Transport protects your coatings. It does not protect the counter.

You told us your nearest real competitor is in Dublin, because paint is a pain to move. That is right, and it is a genuine moat — nobody is freighting drums of Jotun to Belfast to undercut you by a few pounds a litre.

It does not cover brushes, rollers, overalls and masking. Those are light, cheap to post, and three English websites sell them with a price on the screen. That is the part of your trade that is actually exposed, and it is the part with no prices on your own site.

And you are the only firm of the six running no analytics at all.

Where The Threat Is Real, And Where It Is Not
Your moat — and it is a real one

Coatings are heavy, often hazardous, and expensive to freight to Northern Ireland. A contractor doing 1,000 square metres is not ordering his system from a website in England and waiting on a pallet. He rings you, and he will keep ringing you.

Nothing in this document suggests otherwise, and nobody should be telling you that your coatings business is under threat from a website. It is not.

The exposed part — small, light, repeat
Brushes, rollers, frames post easily — sold online by all three Overalls, masking, paper post easily — sold online by all three Single tins and touch-ups post easily — sold online by all three Bulk coating systems protected by freight cost and hazard rules

Your over-the-counter trade is roughly two hundred pounds an order. That is exactly the size of basket an English website is built to win, and exactly the size a contractor will order online at seven in the morning rather than drive to Albertbridge Road for.

Not one of those orders is available on your website today, because none of your 571 products carries a price or a buy button.

Industrial Coatings Ltd is running a Google Ads conversion tag, which means they are buying search traffic and measuring what it returns. Some of those searches are made in Northern Ireland by people who want a price on something small.

What They Cannot Do
And this is the whole reason you are still here after 27 years

Not one of those English websites will work out how much paint the job needs.

They will sell a contractor twenty litres of whatever he types into the search box. They will not tell him it is the wrong system for that substrate, that he has underestimated the area, or that he needs a primer first. They cannot walk the site. They cannot be rung on a Tuesday when it is going wrong.

You do all of that, and you do not charge for it. It is the reason a contractor comes to you instead of typing “Jotun” into Google — and it is completely absent from your website, which today looks like a catalogue that happens not to have prices.

The two local firms cannot do it either, on the evidence of their own sites. Neither Barron & Brennan nor IPC Coatings puts a survey, a specification or a take-off service in front of a visitor. Nobody in this table is claiming the thing you actually sell.

And what you sell is not paint. It is the specification. You survey the building, write the specification, and it goes out to three or four contractors to price. Whichever one wins the job comes back and buys the material from you. You are paid regardless of who wins, because you wrote the document they are all pricing against.

That is a genuinely strong position and there is nothing about it anywhere on your website — no survey page, no specification page, nothing telling a building owner that this is a service they can ask you for.

Two different problems, and they need different answers. Your coatings are protected by freight and always will be. Your counter trade is not protected by anything — it is small, light, repeat, and sold online by three firms who publish prices. And the specification work, which is the most valuable thing you do, is claimed by nobody at all, including you.

What We Would Do About It
  • Claim the survey and the specification publicly. Nobody in this table advertises it. It is the thing you already do, it is why contractors buy from you, and no website in England can copy it.
  • Install analytics. Every competitor has it. It is free and you are the only firm of six flying blind.
Then
  • Prices on tools and consumables — roughly £200 a time, and the only part of your trade that freight does not protect
  • A specification and CPD page — you already deliver the seminars and host 25 brochures; none of these five firms shows a specifier anything comparable
  • Keep coatings as an enquiry — that is your advantage, not your weakness, and it is the only reason a contractor rings rather than clicks

Nobody can freight your coatings. Anybody can post a brush.

And not one firm in this table — local or national — offers to survey a building and write the specification. That is the business you are actually in, and it is the one thing your website never mentions.
