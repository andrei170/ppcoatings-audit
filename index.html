First, The Thing You Are Right About
We are not going to sell you something that does not fit

You told our team that you are a supplier — that you handle enquiries for the people who do the paint jobs — and that most of what a marketing agency does therefore cannot help you. That is a fair read, and on the usual version of the pitch you are correct.

Chasing homeowners would be wrong for you. Your customers are contractors, applicators, facilities teams and specifiers. They already know coatings. They are not going to be persuaded by an advert.

So everything below is about three things only: the online counter you have already built and not switched on, the specification work you already do and cannot measure, and the 27 years of proof sitting on your site under a filename that says “test”.

Nothing in here asks you to become something you are not.

Finding 1 — You Have Built A Shop And Left It Locked
Read from your live site and sitemap, 7 September 2026
Products published 571 Product categories 27 Products showing a price 0 Products with a buy button 0 WooCommerce installed yes Cart page live yes /cart/ returns a working page Account page live yes /my-account/ returns a working page Order attribution tracking installed WooCommerce sourcebuster is running

The whole till is fitted and the drawer is empty. You have paid for a full ecommerce system, catalogued 571 products across 27 categories, and a customer who finds a paint brush on your site cannot buy it.

Look at what is actually in that catalogue. Contractor brushes. Wire cage roller frames. Chipping hammers. Brown paper rolls. Disposable overalls. Drill mixing paddles. Squeegee holders. These are consumables. The same contractor buys them again next month, and the month after.

That is the easiest revenue in your business and the least worth a phone call. A contractor ordering forty pounds of brushes has to ring your counter, and somebody on your team has to stop what they are doing and take that order by hand — at seven staff, that is not a small cost.

The coatings themselves are a different matter and we would leave them alone. Jotun and Remmers systems need a specification, a substrate and a conversation. Nobody should be buying those from a dropdown, and we are not suggesting it. The tools and consumables are not that — and they are the ones already listed with no price.

Finding 2 — Nothing On The Site Is Measured
Searched the full served page source
Google Analytics not present Google Tag Manager not present Google Ads tag not present Meta pixel not present Call tracking not present Scripts actually served: jQuery, WooCommerce, the site theme, Gravity Forms and Google reCAPTCHA. Nothing else.

There is no analytics of any kind on the website. Not a broken one, not an old one — none.

This matters far more for you than it would for a contractor, because of what you sell. You carry eight brands and 27 product categories. You cannot see which of them people come looking for.

Which brand gets searched for most. Which category people browse and then leave. Which of your 571 products get looked at every week and which have never been opened. That is stock intelligence, not marketing — and for a distributor holding inventory it is worth more than any advert.

WooCommerce is already recording where orders come from — the order attribution script is live on your site. Nothing is reading it.

Analytics is free, and it only records from the day it is switched on. Every week without it is a week of history that cannot be recovered later.

Finding 3 — Royal Hillsborough Castle Is Filed Under “test-1”
Your live case study URLs, exactly as published
/case-studies/test-1/ → Royal Hillsborough Castle Gates /case-studies/test-8/ → Commercial Roof Renovation /case-studies/test-case-study/ → published and live /case-studies/interior-waterproofing/ ← the only one named properly Case studies published in total 4 in 27 years of trading

You have supplied coatings for the gates at Royal Hillsborough Castle — the King's official residence in Northern Ireland — and the page is called test-1.

Three of your four case studies are still sitting on the placeholder web addresses the site builder used while they were setting the section up. They were never renamed, and they are live and public right now.

Two separate things are wrong here and only one of them is technical.

The technical one: a page called test-1 tells Google nothing. A page called royal-hillsborough-castle-gates-coating tells it exactly what the page is about. That is a ten-minute fix.

The one that actually costs you money: four case studies in 27 years. You are the firm the specifier has to be convinced by, and a royal residence is about as strong as a reference gets in Northern Ireland. It is buried, unnamed, and it is one of only four.

Finding 4 — The Specification Side Is Invisible
Services listed on your own site, with no page built for any of them
Site surveys link only, no page content Specifications link only, no page content Demonstrations link only, no page content CPD presentations link only, no page content Brochures hosted 25 a genuine library, and a real asset

This is the most valuable thing on your website and it is four words in a menu.

You offer CPD presentations. That means you go into architects' and engineers' offices and teach. In the specification trade that is not marketing collateral — it is the whole route to market. The specifier writes your system into the document, and then every contractor bidding that job has to come to you.

An architect looking for a CPD on protective coatings, intumescent systems or resin floors will search for exactly that. There is no page on your site for them to find.

Your 25-brochure library is the same story. It is a genuinely useful resource that specifiers want, and there is nothing capturing who downloads what. A specifier downloading your intumescent brochure is the single most valuable visitor your site gets, and today they arrive and leave without you knowing they existed.

Finding 5 — The Site Has Been Left Alone
Freshness, read from your sitemap
News articles published 3 Most recent news article 19 December 2024 roughly 20 months ago Most prominent news item "Christmas 2024" Most recent portfolio item 29 January 2026

The most visible dated thing on your website is a Christmas message from 2024.

To a contractor who already knows you, that means nothing — they ring the counter regardless. To a specifier checking you out before writing you into a document, a site that looks last-touched two Christmases ago is a small reason to hesitate, and they will never tell you that was the reason.

This is the least urgent finding in the document and we are including it for completeness rather than alarm. It is a symptom of the same thing as Findings 1 and 3: the site was built well, handed over, and nobody has been back through it since.

What Is Genuinely Good
And there is a lot of it — this is a well-built site and a serious business
  • 571 products, properly catalogued across 27 categories. That is a real piece of work and it is already done. The hard part of an online counter is the catalogue, and you have it.
  • Eight brands, named and given their own pages — Jotun, Remmers, Rust-Oleum, Tor, Flexcrete, Watco. Contractors search by brand and you are findable for them.
  • A 25-brochure technical library. Very few distributors bother, and specifiers genuinely use these.
  • A working enquiry form and a tap-to-call number — 028 9073 1333 — both functioning correctly on mobile.
  • Email on your own domain, which a surprising number of firms still get wrong.
  • The site is well built and fast on a phone. This is not a cheap template job and it does not need replacing.
  • Twenty-seven years trading, same family, same address. That is the part nobody can shortcut, and it is already yours.

Nothing in this document needs a new website. The asset is built. It needs switching on, measured, and its best work brought to the front.

Fix First — This Week, And Most Of It Is Free
  • Install analytics. Free, and it starts counting the day it goes on. Everything else is guesswork until this exists.
  • Rename the three “test” case study addresses. Ten minutes, and it stops a royal residence being filed as a placeholder.
  • Put Royal Hillsborough Castle on the homepage. It is your strongest single reference and it is three clicks deep.
  • Ask your best twenty contractors for a Google review. You have 27 years of goodwill and nothing published.
Build Next
  • Switch on prices and ordering for tools and consumables only. Leave every coating as an enquiry. This is the whole first phase and it touches nothing about how you sell systems.
  • A trade account signup — contractor prices behind a login, which is how your customers already expect to buy.
  • A real page for CPD presentations and specifications, written for architects and engineers rather than contractors.
  • Capture on the brochure library, so you know which specifier downloaded which system and can follow it up.
  • More case studies. You have 27 years of them and four published.

The order matters. Analytics first, because it costs nothing and every decision after it is better informed. The shop second, because it is already built and paid for. Everything else can wait until those two are earning.
